# MARS-LoRa — research readiness and integrated simulation status

## Deliverable inventory (2026-11-21)
- **Vercel browser flight console:** physics-capable MuJoCo WASM; real visual CAD meshes for Bitcraze Crazyflie 2 and Skydio X2; deterministic 3D city + industrial equipment with physical collisions; waypoint route controller; 32 horizontal + 32 downward-canted rangefinder beams; a LiDAR-based forward-stop supervisor; GPS-denied planar occupancy mapping.
- **Native physics benchmark:** `mujoco_sim/validate_mission.py` integrates both airframes and the same deterministic urban buildings at 250 Hz, with controls updated every 20 ms. Logs true positions, tracked lookahead target error, clearance, body tilt and contacts at each 4-ms physics step.
- **LoRa research:** `experiments/replay_mujoco_radio.py` uses each real MuJoCo CSV to determine simulated radio path geometry. The ray-AABB test intersects exactly the same 72 deterministic city structures and industrial fixtures. 480 trials = 2 airframes × 2 receiver placements × 2 shadowing levels × 20 seeds × 3 radio policies. Receiver is ground-mounted at (230,220,5) or elevated at (230,220,120). Radio assumptions remain synthetic and hardware unverified.
- **Paper:** `paper/paper.tex` compiles with the other seeded and factorial radio experiments; real figures are made from `results/crosslayer/summary.csv`, not manually drawn to look favorable.
- **Source provenance:** Google DeepMind MuJoCo Menagerie, `bitcraze_crazyflie_2` MIT and `skydio_x2` Apache-2.0. Meshes are included in `public/assets/` with their licenses.

## Verified deterministic flight checks
Latest full native MuJoCo validation (one industrial survey trajectory per drone):
- Skydio-model: 7/7 waypoints, 90.57 s, 0 contact steps, min clearance 1.00 m, XY RMS error 0.878 m, maximum tilt 6.58°.
- Crazyflie-model: 7/7 waypoints, 105.35 s, 0 contact steps, min clearance 1.24 m, XY RMS error 0.926 m, maximum tilt 8.48°.

These are **one-run** verification metrics. They are not confidence intervals and do not constitute complete robustness testing. They are stored in `results/flight-validation/summary.json`; generated by CI.

## Radio-trace coupling methodology
1. Integrate a flight in native MuJoCo, logging time-indexed XY and altitude at approximately 10 Hz.
2. Replay each flight at 4-Hz radio-event sampling without inventing a kinematic trajectory.
3. Cast a 3D segment from the current physical UAV location to an elevated or ground receiver through the city AABBs.
4. If intersected, apply 17 dB additional modeled NLOS path loss. Add log-distance path loss exponent 4 and AR(1) shadowing with seeded randomness.
5. Generate telemetry every 2 seconds and a critical alert every 25 seconds. Compare fixed SF9, adaptive SF and adaptive-SF with safety-message priority.
6. Compute PDR, timely-critical ratio, P95 AoI, NLOS fraction, outage fraction, TX airtime and **modeled** TX energy. Store all trials, source code and a manifest in GitHub.
7. Only after sufficient matched seeds, additional flight paths, and measured-radio calibration should the project describe improved reliability as a general result.

### Research limitations still blocking a journal-level claim
- **Progress (2026-11-21):** A new 3D risk-constrained A* planner now creates two collision-safe routes over the same static city map: 54 m geometric shortest versus 80 m RF-aware. Both are independently executed by native MuJoCo for two airframes, with 0 contact steps in deterministic trials. 240 additional matched-seed simulated-LoRa trials compare shortest+fixed SF, shortest+adaptive SF/priority and RF-aware+adaptive SF/priority. The actual *fully joint route/SF optimizer* (including QoS constraints, sensor uncertainty and on-line re-planning) remains unfinished. The current radio-aware planner uses an expected SF9 quality proxy, and LoRa adaptation occurs during post-flight radio replay.
- Native flight controller still has approx. 0.9 m lateral RMS tracking error, and control gains/IMU/drag are not hardware calibrated.
- GPS-denied SLAM is a basic planar occupancy grid with local scan matching. It has no 3D loop closure, multi-sensor fusion, calibrated IMU bias model or independent accuracy evidence from paired flight runs.
- The sensor obstacle response brakes and hovers; it does not perform robust online 3D replanning or certify collision freedom. The simulator-only ground-truth supervisor is separate and would not exist onboard.
- Actual LoRa PHY/link tuning, duty cycle, SF handshake, external RF surveys and flight-control robustness over random weather seeds are not validated. LoRa must remain outside the high-frequency stabilization loop.
- City scene is reproducible and **synthetic**, not yet sourced from a real-world GIS database. OpenStreetMap import would require georeferencing, attribution, real elevations and coordinate transformation; an arbitrarily chosen city should not be presented as field measured.

## New measured 3D route planning experiment

- Source: `mars_lora/rf_astar.py` (collision-guarded grid A*), `experiments/rf_navigation.py` (real MuJoCo flight + matched radio replay), `analysis/paired_rf_navigation.py` (95% descriptive intervals), and `public/src/rf_route_planner.js` (interactive browser A* missions).
- Scientific fairness: S1 vs R2 holds packet scheduling and adaptive SF policy fixed and changes only the executed route; identical seeded time-series fading per comparator. **Limitations:** route durations differ, fading is time-indexed rather than a consistent spatial random field, planning and radio evaluation reuse the same obstruction model, and the obstacle map is exact ground truth rather than discovered SLAM occupancy.
- Actual results at 4 dB synthetic shadowing, 20 seeds, Skydio-based vehicle: S0 shortest/fixed PDR 0.104, S1 shortest/adaptive 0.432, R2 RF route/adaptive 0.784; mean alert deadline ratios 0.050, 0.500, 0.838. R2 needs 118.51 s versus S1 105.23 s, but modeled TX energy declines 6.29 to 1.35 J due to improved line of sight. Both executed routes had zero MuJoCo contact events. Pairwise 95% normal intervals conditional on this scene are saved to `results/rf_navigation/paired_effects.csv`.
- Critical negative issue: these improvements may overfit the **same simplified ray-AABB shadow model used during planning and evaluation**. Hardware or an independent propagation model is essential to validate the tradeoff rather than just optimizing model assumptions.

## Next decision: implement the full novelty
**Hypothesis:** Under GPS-denied localization uncertainty, a UAV can improve deadline-constrained LoRa alert delivery by choosing a collision-safe 3D route and transmission policy jointly, subject to minimum clearance, bounded AoI, radio energy and flight-time constraints.

Baseline 1: constant safe altitude and SF9.
Baseline 2: collision-aware shortest path from a 3D occupancy/voxel grid.
Baseline 3: same collision-aware route with adaptive SF and priority queue.
Candidate: RF-aware route + adaptive SF + priority + predictive stop/relay positioning, with conservative behavior under localization uncertainty.

Use identical generated alerts, seeded fading *spatial field*, equivalent mission geometry and real MuJoCo executed trajectories for every comparison. Measure 3D clearance/contact, localization ATE/RPE, vehicle energy/time, PDR, AoI, deadline satisfaction and radio energy. Run >=20 randomized environmental cases and report confidence intervals/negative cases.
